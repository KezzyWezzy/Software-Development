LDS & Hybrid Sales Plan

Lead with the LDS on every liquid line. Add drone docks where geography, HCA exposure or false-alarm costs justify them. This plan sizes both offers from PHMSA's 2025 annual report data and says which one to lead with for each kind of operator.

Which offer to lead with

Decision guide

LDS alone

Line under 10 miles inside or next to a plant, terminal or refinery. A drone adds little where staff can walk the line. Quote the LDS only.

LDS first

10–500 miles, mostly road-accessible, little HCA. Sell the LDS now. Write the drone into the proposal as phase 2 so the option is on file.

Hybrid

Marsh, offshore or water crossings, or more than half the miles in HCAs. Lead with the hybrid. Open with a 1-dock pilot on the worst segment.

Hybrid add-on

500+ miles with an existing LDS vendor. Don't try to replace their LDS first. Offer alarm-check docks on HCA segments that work alongside whatever LDS they run, then compete for the LDS at renewal.

Signals that move an LDS-only customer to hybrid
  • More than a few unconfirmed alarms a year, each ending in a shutdown or a truck dispatch
  • A segment in marsh, water or another place that's hard to reach
  • A new HCA designation, a nearby construction boom, or a recent incident
  • A manned aircraft patrol contract up for renewal

First 90 days

Two tracks in parallel

WEEKS 1–2

Set up both tracks

  • LDS track: a standard short-line package (fixed scope, fixed price, set install time) and a sample uncertainty budget to show prospects
  • Hybrid track: a simulator demo where an LDS alarm sends the drone; ask Genesis for a meeting
  • Re-run Texas with the corrected tool to rank Texas operators by HCA
WEEKS 3–6

Short-line LDS deals and hybrid pilots

  • Call the Texas and Louisiana plant and refinery lists. Aim for two or three short-line LDS sales as references
  • Offer Louisiana marsh operators a 90-day pilot: LDS on the line plus 1 dock on the worst segment
  • Start the FAA application to fly beyond line of sight
WEEKS 6–10

Independents: LDS first

  • Proposals for the whole system with a priced phase-2 dock option
  • Use the short-line references in these pitches
WEEKS 8–13

Install, measure, expand

  • First pilot dock goes live. Record how long it takes to confirm each alarm
  • Take the results to the large operators as the alarm-check add-on
Why liquid operators buy now
  • PHMSA's 2019 liquid rule extended the LDS requirement in 49 CFR 195.444 beyond HCAs to most onshore liquid lines, with existing lines due in 2024. Confirm how it applies to each operator
  • API RP 1130 (CPM) and API RP 1175 (leak detection program) are what auditors check against. An API 1149 uncertainty budget makes the alarm threshold defensible
  • Small operators often met the deadline with a basic system and now have nuisance alarms, which is an opening to replace it
Data notes
  • Source: PHMSA annual report data, 2025 report year
  • Louisiana HCA miles come from Part B, split across states by mileage for multi-state operators. Texas HCA is pending a re-run
  • Dock costs are planning estimates, not quotes. LDS pricing is yours to set
  • Gas operators are left out: CPM on gas lines is a different product. The methane drone covers them in phase 2
