Why a customer pays for a dock (example)

A 100,000 bbl/day crude line that shuts down on an unconfirmed alarm loses about $10–30k an hour. A 4-hour shutdown at $20k an hour costs $80k, which is roughly 3 years of service on one dock. Two avoided shutdowns pay for the dock itself.

What would sink the projection
  • FAA approval to fly beyond line of sight takes longer than a year, which stalls dock revenue
  • Short-line deals need more custom work than the fixed package assumes, so margins fall
  • Large operators renew with their current LDS vendor, so the add-on segment stays small
How the model works
  • New customers each year = pool × won-by-year-5 × ramp (10%, 20%, 25%, 25%, 20%)
  • One-time LDS revenue is booked in the year won; annual fees and dock service continue every year after
  • Hybrid customers buy their docks in the year they sign
  • Operating profit = gross profit − operating costs (staff, sales, overhead), before tax and interest
What's data and what's assumed
  • Data: pool sizes (PHMSA 2025 annual reports; an operator in both states is counted once per state)
