## The strategy

The goal is the video, not a lawsuit. A request with no consequence attached gets filed and
forgotten while the footage overwrites itself, so the packet runs four independent tracks:

1. **Litigation hold / spoliation notice** — the load-bearing piece, and it works whether or
   not anyone cooperates. Once the County has written notice that litigation is reasonably
   anticipated, its duty to preserve attaches. Letting the video overwrite *after* that
   notice exposes it to sanctions and an adverse-inference instruction. That turns "we
   deleted it" from an escape into a liability.
2. **Chapter 119 public records request** — MIA is owned and operated by Miami-Dade County,
   a political subdivision, so its records are presumptively public.
3. **Conditional release (Exhibit A)** — the carrot. Full release of all claims, no money
   asked, but it is void unless conforming video is delivered by the deadline. Nothing is
   waived unless and until they perform.
4. **Stated fallback** — explicit notice of what happens on day 22. The offer is only
   credible if the alternative is.

Because the CZM arrival was international, part of the path through MIA ran through the
federal inspection area. Cameras there belong to **CBP**, not the County, which is why
Document 4 exists as a separate track.

## Before sending — fill in

- **Time window** of the fall (a range, not a single minute — bracket it with the AA 4072
  arrival time and any phone photo timestamps)
- **Location**: concourse, level, nearest gate numbers, a named landmark. "An escalator at
  MIA" is a request they can honestly fail to fill; "the down escalator between Concourse D
  gates 30 and 32" is not.
- Direction of travel; what you observed about the escalator's condition (only what you
  actually saw — write "unknown" rather than guessing)
- Witnesses, responders, badge numbers, employers
- Whether an incident report was made, and by whom
- Injuries and any medical treatment
- The response deadline — 21 calendar days from the mailing date is the recommended default

## Sending

Certified mail with return receipt **and** email, same day, to each recipient. Keep the
green card, delivery confirmation, and sent emails — proof of the receipt date is what makes
the preservation duty enforceable. Calendar the deadline the day you mail.

## Do today, regardless

- Write out everything you remember about the fall, dated
- Preserve boarding passes, AA app screenshots, photos, texts/calls from that day, the
  clothing and shoes worn, and any medical paperwork
- If you haven't been evaluated by a doctor, consider it — delayed documentation is the most
  common weakness in these claims
- Don't post about it publicly; don't give a recorded statement to any insurer or adjuster
  before talking to a lawyer

Airport surveillance systems commonly overwrite on a cycle measured in days or weeks. An
imperfect preservation letter delivered this week beats a polished one delivered next month.
